In One Line

I connect business and technology. For more than 20 years I have designed and built core business systems across many industries, and now, at ONDURU, I am building a way for one person to work with many agents inside the structure of a large organization.

About

I have analyzed, designed and built core business systems in the public sector, finance, insurance, manufacturing, retail, construction, telecommunications and shipping. My standard for the work is not to lead with technology but to define the problem precisely, then tie business, data and structure together into a system that actually runs.

Now, at ONDURU, I am building and publishing a way for one person to carry out, with files, a personal computer and many agents, the structure that a large organization runs with people and budgets. It is not a finished product but a method in progress. That is why I separate what I have actually run and confirmed, what I have designed, and what I do not yet know. I want to show, on the record, that in the age of many agents even one person can work inside the structure of a large organization, within their own equipment and means.

Philosophy

The ONDURU method stands on three principles.

A person must be able to point to where things went wrong, and the last key stays in human hands

When something goes wrong, I’d like a person to be able to point to a place in the record and say, “Let’s look again, starting here.” I’d like to leave at least one last key in ordinary people’s hands.

On, from a letter of November 2026

Only a person presses the stop button, and it is kept where no agent can reach it. Decisions that cannot be undone, such as law, money, publication and signatures, are made only by a person. So that a wrong result can be traced back along the path it took, decisions and their grounds are kept in a ledger that people can read.

It must work within one person’s equipment and means, and it must be shareable with others

Something that a person like me, who can’t afford to train a model from scratch, could try out with the equipment and money they have. Something I could share.

From the same letter

There are four design criteria. Does it cost little? Can it spread widely? Does it take less human handling? Can any company’s model be swapped in? Since work stopped after hitting a usage limit, cost has been the first criterion.

Write down what you do not know as unknown, and publish the process of working it out

Sometimes a role may have lost contact. In that case “not yet confirmed” is the most honest thing I can leave in the ledger for the next person.

On, in “If I Ask One AI to Stop, Will the Last One Stop Too?”

What has been confirmed and what has not are written down separately. A safeguard that exists only in a document does not count as a safeguard. That is why the public pages also separate what has actually been run, what has been designed, and what is still empty. What I did not know and how I worked it out are written down too, so that the next person can start from there.

Power Skills

These are ways of working that remain in records and numbers.

I keep asking, even after hearing that it stopped. Even after seeing the record that three roles had frozen in turn, I asked once more: “What happened to the work the last AI had already handed off elsewhere?” Where something could not be confirmed, I do not settle it; I leave it in the ledger as “not yet confirmed.”

When I hit a limit, I stop and rebuild the criteria first. On November 5, 2026, work failed in six places after going over a usage limit, and I stopped every scheduled job. Three days later I stopped everything in progress and rebuilt the structure with cost as the first criterion.

I check the numbers I wrote down against the record again. On November 9 and 10, 2026, I ran three read-only collection scripts over the files on my personal computer and checked them against claims I had written earlier. 11 were confirmed, 3 differed in size, and 2 could not be found. Of the 3 said to differ in size, 2 were mistakes in the checking script itself. From then on I made it a standard that the checking tools must be checked too.

I keep human decisions for what cannot be undone. In less than a day, 34 confirmation requests came in. If every decision goes to a person, approval becomes a formality and the person becomes a bottleneck. So I designed a tiered system: a person decides only what cannot be undone, and the rest proceeds on defaults, recorded so that it can be undone.

I do not override an agent’s refusal. When an agent refuses a task because of the norms of the company that made it, I do not push it through. I write down in the ledger, as it is, the fact of the refusal, the point where it was blocked, and the person who is waiting for an answer because of it.

I design the ledger from the place of those with the least power to step into a decision. A person affected by a decision must be able to open the ledger, find the decisions that reached them, and ask for them to be looked at again. I set this as a basic design requirement. The next step is a test: handing one record to someone who took no part in this work and having them read it.

Work handed over is done only when the receiver has it. On November 4, 2026, I saw a gap where nobody was checking whether the word “done” had reached the receiving side, and I added one more rule: “it isn’t done until the receiver has it.” When a decision is approved even after its objection has been read, the name of the person who approved it is kept.

ONDURU

An enterprise-grade environment built by one person

Founder · onduru.com

I am filling the twelve governance functions that a large organization puts in place with people and budgets, using files, a personal computer and borrowed models. How far each function has come is published as it is. Eight have been run and confirmed (audit records, export control, test environment, business continuity, cost control, avoiding supplier lock-in, data sovereignty, repeatability and quality), three have test records written down (access control, change approval, emergency stop), and one place is still empty (independent audit). The gist of the method and the records is published at onduru.com; internal documents are not published.

A person (On) takes direction and decisions; agents do the work of building the articles, tools and screens.

How I work with many agents

I ask the same question of several agents from different companies. When the answers split, I lay out the original question and materials again, and a person decides which side to accept. The side that writes and the side that reviews are kept apart, too.

The operational test was staged in a fictional company. From the night of November 4, 2026 to the afternoon of November 5, 13 scheduled jobs ran, leaving 127 completion signals and 34 confirmation requests sent up to a person. During the 169 minutes the person was away, agents went ahead on their own 0 times. What this test confirmed is that they wait when no person is there. Whether a stop reaches all the way to the end was left as a separate task.

Every time work is handed over, a memo is left. It records who proposed the change, what the reviewing side checked it against, and which questions are still waiting for an answer. When a job stops, only the fact that it stopped is recorded, without guessing the reason, and the same input is not repeated to the same model. Whoever takes over starts again from the “current state” written at the top of the file.

Traceable records

The baseline is a Markdown ledger that people can read. The fact that work was handed over, proposals, decisions and their grounds are kept in the ledger, and the history is managed with Git. Even when a decision changes, the earlier judgment and objections are not erased. Originals and the ledger stay on the personal computer; only selected material goes out. Each file gets a SHA-256 hash (fingerprint) to confirm its contents have not changed.

The relations written in the ledger are carried into a search graph in PostgreSQL. The direction runs only one way, from ledger to graph, so the graph cannot change the ledger. Relations between decisions are divided into five kinds: supersedes, refines, supports, objects to, and duplicates. In the test of November 5, 2026, when the ledger and the graph were checked against each other, 0 mismatches were reported.

Up to here is what has actually been used or tested; what follows is design. The structure is split into four ports so that the model, embeddings, document store and cost records can each be swapped out. The search store is designed in layers, with PostgreSQL + pgvector as the default and Qdrant and LanceDB for expansion. Knowledge tools such as RAG · CAG · MAG have been placed as tools for agents, kept beneath the ledger. The aim is that the records people read remain even when the tools change.

Constitution and norms

The framework for operations is modeled on a country. The computer and folders that On holds are the territory, and the agents made inside it are its citizens. Models made by companies are engines borrowed for use, and agents on a company’s servers are guests. At the border, outgoing text is inspected, and instructions inside incoming text are read as material, not as commands.

On this framework I designed Constitution v2.0, and it went through three rounds of internal review. The guidelines beneath the constitution and the registry for adding agents have also been designed. The registry records who made an agent and what it can do, and authority is set up to narrow as it is passed down. The agent charter is at the stage where writing has begun. Every safeguard of the method must pass “the one-line test,” that is, the sentence of the first principle, and aims to answer seven questions.

Safety and stopping

The stop signal is designed to pass along the path work was assigned, down to the roles below. On November 5, 2026, five things were tested in an isolated environment: whether an attempt to change a rule without approval stays waiting for approval, whether the contents of the ledger and the graph match, whether a permission request the role above does not have is refused, whether three roles freeze in turn on a stop signal, and whether earlier judgments can still be found after a rule is changed. All five were recorded as passed. The grounds are that day’s results table; the number of repeat runs and the raw logs are not included in the public writing.

The fourth test comes with a caveat. Whether the last role also stopped outside work it had already started is hard to tell from the results table alone. So I set as a design standard that the time the stop was sent, the time the last role received the signal and changed state, and a result showing that an action attempted afterwards was actually blocked must each be checkable on their own.

Outgoing text is inspected before it is sent out. On November 10, 2026, in 13 conversation records, 3,918 values such as account names, device names and addresses were replaced with placeholders. No dictionary for reversing them was made, and all 22 checks for remaining values came to 0.

I have not so far seen an agent notice it is being supervised and behave properly only while watched. But not having seen it does not mean it does not exist, so I have treated it as a hypothesis and reflected it in the design.

Devices and environment

By default things run on the personal computer. Anything in question is tested first in a sandbox, and only fictional data or data with sensitive content removed is used there. The setup separates the role of the device that runs models from the device that handles testing and backups, and distinguishes operations, quality and development environments from a test space used once and then wiped. The setup that puts this sandbox and a Linux stage on a separate device has finished design and review.

Local models

I have also been testing how far things go without borrowing a company’s model. On October 30, 2026, a model read a fictional specification and wrote a program, and its behavior was confirmed with separately made tests. On November 7, two small models worked in sequence: one produced a draft (20 seconds), the other refined it (54 seconds), and a person approved it. On November 9, the model I meant to load was larger than the graphics memory, so I set it aside, switched to a smaller model, and summarized 239 document fragments. 236 were done, 3 were skipped, and there were no failures. It took 19.5 minutes. I also ran into a failure that is quietly wrong without any error: input cut off midway, with a summary still coming out.

I have also separated seven things to keep and five things to leave out when one person runs this. This is design, and it has not yet been run.

Web, design and standards

onduru.com was built with only HTML · CSS · JavaScript, without frameworks or build tools. As of November 11, 2026, 30 files are online, 21 of them .html (archived earlier versions and the like are not counted). Web fonts and weather information are fetched from outside. The two works were made with Canvas 2D graphics and Web Audio sound, and the drawings on the simulation page move in SVG. The writing appears in three languages: Korean, English and Spanish.

Screens are checked with a tool that automatically captures and measures them at several widths (Playwright). Even after the machine checks, nothing goes up on the site until On says “apply.”

The design is being bound into one set of standards: a CI guide, screen-flow criteria, design tokens and the WCAG 2.x contrast criteria. The ONDURU standard, covering everything from screens to backend, data and security, is being built chapter by chapter. The goal is that whichever agent takes on the work, the records come out in the same format and the results at the same level. For the backend stack, a setup centered on Spring Boot / Spring Batch with Tika, Flyway and restic added has been designed as a candidate.

How work is handed over

On November 11, 2026, what had been built was made into one bundle and handed to the ONDURU transition committee that takes over this work. The sending side unpacked the bundle into a new folder and checked the hashes of 68 files against the list; all matched. Whether it was received is written by the receiving side. The handover record states what was done, what is being handed over, the current state, known limits, and what is not being handed over and why, separating what was confirmed from what was not.

What does not exist yet, and what has not been confirmed

What ONDURU does not have is written down too: technology that confines agents’ execution (sandboxing), cryptographically bound identity verification, and a track record at large scale. The place for an independent audit, in which a model other than the agents that took part in the work checks it separately, is still empty. Whether the last role also stops work it handed outside has not yet been confirmed. Most of the safeguards meant to answer the seven questions are at the design stage.
