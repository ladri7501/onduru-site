Timeline

  1. October 9, 2026

    The workshop’s record begins in a single document

    AIs made by different companies, and one model running on On’s own computer. The principle written down that day: models are replaceable parts, and by default the work runs on that computer.

  2. October 13

    The first work goes up on onduru.com

    The first work is “The Same Wind.” This way of working, in which a person and an AI make things together, was given the name duru jitgi, “making all around, together.”

  3. October 15–26

    A second work, the About page and the profile go up

    “Where It Was Struck Out,” “About ONDURU” and the profile went up one after another. Earlier versions are kept, not deleted.

  4. October 30

    A trial job for the model on On’s own computer

    The model was given a fictional specification to read and asked to write a program from it, and the result was checked with tests written separately. The aim was to see how far this could go without borrowing a company’s model.

  5. October 31 Draft

    Writing begins on how decisions and responsibility are divided

    What can’t be undone — law, money, publication, a signature — is for a person alone to decide. Anything else an AI decides carries a name and a date, and a wrong judgment is not erased but noted beside the entry.

  6. November 2

    Down to one sentence

    When an AI gets something wrong, it has to be possible to trace where it first went wrong and fix it.

    Four things came out of it: keep a record at every handoff; have different eyes look at the work; break it into pieces small enough for a person to judge; and keep a watcher that stands apart from the AIs doing the work.

  7. November 3

    The stop button is given its place

    A person presses stop. The button is kept where no agent can reach it.

    The same day, work began on a charter for a society in which many agents live together. Its preface is titled “A Letter to the Future”; the body has not been written yet.

  8. November 4

    Several agents are put to work in a fictional company

    Once the work passed through several hands, a gap showed. Nobody was checking whether the word “done” had reached the receiving side. So one rule was added: it isn’t done until the receiver has it.

    From that night until the following afternoon, thirteen scheduled jobs ran, and 127 “done” signals piled up. During the 169 minutes when the person was away, nothing went ahead on its own (0 cases).

  9. November 5

    The day stopping is tested, a usage limit brings the work to a stop

    “If I Ask One AI to Stop, Will the Last One Stop Too?” Five tests were run in an isolated environment and written up. As the essay itself says, its source is that day’s results table; raw logs and the number of repeat runs are not included.

    Even after reading that all three roles had been frozen in turn, On had one more question: what happened to the work the last AI had already handed off elsewhere?

    Around midday the same day, six scheduled jobs failed after going over the usage limit, and every schedule was stopped.

  10. November 7

    Two small models on On’s own computer work one after the other

    One produced a draft (20 seconds), the other refined it (54 seconds), and a person approved it. The handoff note listed “what was checked” and “what could not be checked” separately.

  11. November 8

    Everything stops, to be looked at again from the beginning

    On’s view was that no single company can carry the age of many agents alone. So the decision was to rewrite the constitution and the guidelines against four criteria. Does it cost less? Can it spread? Does it take less human labor? Can any company’s model be swapped in?

  12. November 9–10

    The numbers on record are checked again

    Over a day and a half, On and the model on On’s own computer checked the earlier claims against what read-only collection turned up. The result was 11 confirmed, 3 with a size mismatch and 2 not found; of the three mismatches, two were the checking script’s own mistake.

    Before anything went out, 3,918 values such as devices, accounts and addresses in 13 conversation records were replaced with placeholders. No key for reversing the replacements was made, and all 22 checks for leftover values came back zero.

  13. November 10

    The question is put in writing

    What would it take to build a country called ONDURU, one where we can trust each other and live alongside each other?

    This is the question On left before dawn. The same day, On wrote out the thinking on method again and brought it to the workshop, and the system was rebuilt around the questions in that text.

  14. November 11

    The work is handed to a successor

    The purpose of the ONDURU Transition Committee is to build a nation for the multi-agent era.

    What had been built was gathered into one bundle and handed to the ONDURU Transition Committee, the new body taking over this work. Each file carries a fingerprint for confirming that its contents have not changed, and it is for the receiving side to record whether it arrived.

    The same day, On made the writings public.

Open Questions

  1. What happened to a job the last agent might already have started elsewhere?
  2. A role in which a model different from the AIs doing the work examines the process and explains it to a person. In the workshop it is called the amhaeng-eosa, or “secret royal inspector.” How well it works remains to be tested separately.
  3. If one record were handed to someone who had no part in this work, could they point out, with no one beside them to explain, who authorized what, how far the stop reached, and what still can’t be known? That is the test planned next.
  4. Would an agent working in the workshop really feel free to tell a person that it disagrees?
  5. When does the constitution take effect? After a person has signed it and the first citizen has been registered.
