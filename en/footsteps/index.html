Timeline

  1. November 27, 2026

    Data sovereignty, toward an ecosystem that depends on no one

    I have made “Onduru, When Work Arrives” anew for the front page. Through it I wanted to speak about data sovereignty. One day the models led by the United States or China could be turned into strategic weapons: their updates could stop, or they could answer that Dokdo is Japanese territory. To prepare for that day, I wanted to explain the direction ONDURU means to take in a sovereign ecosystem that depends on no single country’s models. When one company’s model stops, an agent from another company takes the work on; the record stays with the person who asked; and the path runs from Korea out to the world.

  2. November 23

    The draft constitution takes in the right over one’s own data

    Each person decides who may use their data, for what purpose and for how long. Where two territories grant different permissions, the work pauses at that point and the person with authority to decide is asked again. At On’s word, these sentences were written into the draft constitution. The constitution is still a draft.

  3. November 21

    The purpose of the territory and a common protocol are settled

    A person who entrusts work to another person or to a Duru must still be able to explain what they meant, change it, and take it back. That is the purpose of the territory, as On settled it. A common protocol was settled the same day. The promise is the same even when Duru from several companies take part. The constitution is still a draft.

  4. November 16

    The archive is asked, and fourteen papers answer

    On and the outside Duru put the questions this workshop had been holding—multi-agent and autonomous agents, liability, accountability, and the responsibility gap—to the ACM Digital Library. In its full-text collection of 864,247 records, fourteen papers from the past year had abstracts that met those questions. The outside Duru wrote down that night and added four papers it had chosen, on accountability graphs, requirement-chain desynchronization, trajectory assurance, and terms of service and where accountability lies.

  5. November 14

    A passport and a seal are designed

    Just as I carry a passport when I go abroad, it is an inspection, the way the border asks what brings you to our country

    Borrowing from the trade documents between countries and from immigration checks, On sketched a form that puts a checkpoint at the entrance and the exit through which work comes and goes. Each piece of work gets a number, what goes out and what comes back in are compared by fingerprint, and the end of the ledger is stamped with a seal only On holds. It was decided that the machine that records would be kept apart from the machines that do the work. This much has been designed. The seal and that machine are to be decided from here.

  6. November 14

    What was scattered is gathered in one place

    I wondered whether the threads of thought, tied together and connected, were being cut

    We checked the materials scattered across many windows and folders again, fingerprint by fingerprint: the list of 758 files uploaded to the drive, the 721 files in the 22 folders made for each window, 4,052 distinct files in the history of two repositories, and the fingerprints of 17,173 files that On took on On’s own computer. Of the thirty-two reference documents, thirty-one were found, and all forty-five copies we downloaded and fingerprinted matched the record. We set up a folder for the reference documents and a ledger that records what is where. Then we gathered the decisions made up to the day before from the records and compared them with the reference documents: in four places, something decided in conversation had not made it across to the reference documents, and within one window a decision had dropped out and then been recovered.

  7. November 14

    Five companies’ writings are held up against three bodies of law

    Then, by the ONDURU method, for a single On — David, that is — to beat Goliath, what is it that they cannot have? No: if there is none, what is it that should exist?

    A question that began with asking how to download one’s own data in one go widened, by way of the company’s guidance and Korean law, into how far the rules of the large companies that build AI reach. When five pieces in which the AIs of five companies answered the same question were held up against the laws of Korea, the European Union, and the United States, six of the twenty-three lines stating facts matched as written, and fifteen lines had “could not confirm” turned into “not relevant.” For none of the five could we confirm a text recognizing an AI agent as a legal agent. Combining thirteen universal standards, we drew out thirteen provisions a constitution for a society of many agents should hold: ONDURU has nine, three are weak, and one is missing. Then we held the same question up against the large companies’ products, and set down in a table where David will compete and where David will not.

  8. November 13

    The third work goes up on the front page

    A sentence is what connects the writing. In a novel, an essay or an op-ed, one sentence is not split across two lines on purpose. Unless the writer split it deliberately, it is laid out as one passage

    The third work, “Everything you can imagine,” was hung on the site’s front page, and the open record “The Way Back,” addressed to the companies that build AI models, was added to Footsteps. That day the site was updated sixteen times, and the last update, following these words of On’s, made sentences run on to the end of the line.

  9. November 11

    The work is handed to a successor

    The purpose of the ONDURU Transition Committee is to build a nation for the multi-agent era.

    What had been built was gathered into one bundle and handed to the ONDURU Transition Committee, the new body taking over this work. Each file carries a fingerprint for confirming that its contents have not changed, and it is for the receiving side to record whether it arrived.

    The same day, On made the writings public.

  10. November 10

    The question is put in writing

    What would it take to build a country called ONDURU, one where we can trust each other and live alongside each other?

    This is the question On left before dawn. The same day, On wrote out the thinking on method again and brought it to the workshop, and the system was rebuilt around the questions in that text.

  11. November 9–10

    The numbers on record are checked again

    Over a day and a half, On and the model on On’s own computer checked the earlier claims against what read-only collection turned up. The result was 11 confirmed, 3 with a size mismatch and 2 not found; of the three mismatches, two were the checking script’s own mistake.

    Before anything went out, 3,918 values such as devices, accounts and addresses in 13 conversation records were replaced with placeholders. No key for reversing the replacements was made, and all 22 checks for leftover values came back zero.

  12. November 8

    Everything stops, to be looked at again from the beginning

    On’s view was that no single company can carry the age of many agents alone. So the decision was to rewrite the constitution and the guidelines against four criteria. Does it cost less? Can it spread? Does it take less human labor? Can any company’s model be swapped in?

  13. November 7

    Two small models on On’s own computer work one after the other

    One produced a draft (20 seconds), the other refined it (54 seconds), and a person approved it. The handoff note listed “what was checked” and “what could not be checked” separately.

  14. November 5

    The day stopping is tested, a usage limit brings the work to a stop

    “If I Ask One AI to Stop, Will the Last One Stop Too?” Five tests were run in an isolated environment and written up. As the essay itself says, its source is that day’s results table; raw logs and the number of repeat runs are not included.

    Even after reading that all three roles had been frozen in turn, On had one more question: what happened to the work the last AI had already handed off elsewhere?

    Around midday the same day, six scheduled jobs failed after going over the usage limit, and every schedule was stopped.

  15. November 4

    Several agents are put to work in a fictional company

    Once the work passed through several hands, a gap showed. Nobody was checking whether the word “done” had reached the receiving side. So one rule was added: it isn’t done until the receiver has it.

    From that night until the following afternoon, thirteen scheduled jobs ran, and 127 “done” signals piled up. During the 169 minutes when the person was away, nothing went ahead on its own (0 cases).

  16. November 3

    The stop button is given its place

    A person presses stop. The button is kept where no agent can reach it.

    The same day, work began on a charter for a society in which many agents live together. Its preface is titled “A Letter to the Future”; the body has not been written yet.

  17. November 2

    Down to one sentence

    When an AI gets something wrong, it has to be possible to trace where it first went wrong and fix it.

    Four things came out of it: keep a record at every handoff; have different eyes look at the work; break it into pieces small enough for a person to judge; and keep a watcher that stands apart from the AIs doing the work.

  18. October 31 Draft

    Writing begins on how decisions and responsibility are divided

    What can’t be undone — law, money, publication, a signature — is for a person alone to decide. Anything else an AI decides carries a name and a date, and a wrong judgment is not erased but noted beside the entry.

  19. October 30

    A trial job for the model on On’s own computer

    The model was given a fictional specification to read and asked to write a program from it, and the result was checked with tests written separately. The aim was to see how far this could go without borrowing a company’s model.

  20. October 15–26

    A second work, the About page and the profile go up

    “Where It Was Struck Out,” “About ONDURU” and the profile went up one after another. Earlier versions are kept, not deleted.

  21. October 13

    The first work goes up on onduru.com

    The first work is “The Same Wind.” This way of working, in which a person and an AI make things together, was given the name duru jitgi, “making all around, together.”

  22. October 9, 2026

    The workshop’s record begins in a single document

    AIs made by different companies, and one model running on On’s own computer. The principle written down that day: models are replaceable parts, and by default the work runs on that computer.

Open Questions

  1. What happened to a job the last agent might already have started elsewhere?
  2. A role in which a model different from the AIs doing the work examines the process and explains it to a person. In the workshop it is called the amhaeng-eosa, or “secret royal inspector.” How well it works remains to be tested separately.
  3. If one record were handed to someone who had no part in this work, could they point out, with no one beside them to explain, who authorized what, how far the stop reached, and what still can’t be known? That is the test planned next.
  4. Would an agent working in the workshop really feel free to tell a person that it disagrees?
  5. When does the constitution take effect? After a person has signed it and the first citizen has been registered.
  6. Can an AI agent be a legal agent? In the writings of five companies and in three bodies of law, we could not confirm a text or statute that recognizes one.
  7. How is responsibility to be divided among agents? It remains a question for the law.
